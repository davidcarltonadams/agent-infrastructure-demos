nonproliferation

In safeguards, what is a 'significant quantity'?

The approximate amount of nuclear material for which the possibility of manufacturing a nuclear explosive device cannot be excluded: 8 kg of plutonium, 25 kg of uranium-235 in highly enriched uranium, or 75 kg of uranium-235 in low-enriched uranium. It allows for process losses and is not a critical mass. Inspection goals are set against it.
Pyramid — for a non-technical listenerIt is the inspectors' unit of concern. Rather than asking whether any material is missing, they ask whether enough could be missing to matter, and they size their checks and their timing to catch that much.
security

What is the two-person rule?

A requirement that at least two authorized people, each able to recognize an incorrect or unauthorized act, be present whenever the material is accessed or a critical task is performed, so no one is ever alone with it.
Pyramid — for a non-technical listenerNo one is ever alone with the material. A second trained person is always there and able to stop a wrong move.
systems

What does it mean for a check to 'fail closed'?

When the check cannot run or returns an error, the system refuses the action instead of letting it through. (In physical security the term is fail-secure.)
Pyramid — for a non-technical listenerWhen the check is down, the system says no by default. An outage costs time, and nothing goes through without the check.

Material Accountancy and Continuity of Knowledge

Safeguards start with bookkeeping. A facility is divided into material balance areas, and the operator records every inventory change in each one: receipts, shipments and measured discards. At each physical inventory the operator measures what is actually there by weighing, sampling and assay, and inspectors verify that inventory independently and compare it with the books.

The gap between the book inventory and the physical inventory is material unaccounted for (MUF); US practice calls it inventory difference. In a bulk-handling plant it is almost never zero, because every measurement carries error and some material stays held up in equipment. What matters is whether the gap is larger than the propagated measurement error (sigma-MUF) can explain, and whether it approaches a significant quantity.

Between inventories, seals and cameras maintain continuity of knowledge: assurance that material already verified has not moved through a path the books do not cover, so it need not be verified again. Accountancy measures quantities; containment and surveillance complement it between measurements. (Synthetic demo lesson.)
