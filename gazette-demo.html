nonproliferation

In safeguards, what is a 'significant quantity'?

The approximate amount of nuclear material from which a nuclear explosive device cannot be ruled out: 8 kg of plutonium, or 25 kg of uranium-235 contained in highly enriched uranium. Inspection goals are set against it.
Pyramid — for a non-technical listenerIt is the inspectors' unit of concern. Rather than asking whether any material is missing, they ask whether enough could be missing to matter, and they size their checks and their timing to catch that much.
security

What is the two-person rule?

A control that requires two authorized people, acting together, for a critical operation, so that no single person can carry it out alone.
Pyramid — for a non-technical listenerThe most dangerous actions need two keys held by two people. One person's mistake, or one person's bad intent, is not enough on its own.
systems

What does it mean for a check to 'fail closed'?

When the check cannot run or returns an error, the system refuses the action instead of letting it through.
Pyramid — for a non-technical listenerWhen the check is down, the system says no by default. An outage costs time, and nothing goes through without the check.

Material Accountancy and Continuity of Knowledge

Safeguards start with bookkeeping. A facility is divided into material balance areas, and the operator records every receipt, shipment and measured loss of nuclear material in each one. At each physical inventory the operator counts what is actually there, and inspectors verify that count against the books.

The difference is called material unaccounted for. It is almost never zero, because every measurement carries error, and a bulk-handling plant measures material in solutions, powders and waste streams. What matters is whether the difference is larger than measurement error can explain.

Between inventories, seals and cameras keep continuity of knowledge: evidence that material did not move through a path the books do not cover. Accountancy measures quantities; containment and surveillance cover the time between counts. (Synthetic demo lesson.)
